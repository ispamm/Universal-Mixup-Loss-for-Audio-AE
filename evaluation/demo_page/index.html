Universal Mixup Loss: Audio Demonstrations

Listening examples for “Universal Mixup Loss: Training Audio Autoencoders to Decode the Positive-Weight Mixing Cone”. Four autoencoders are compared on identical clips at 44.1 kHz: Universal Mixup (ours), SAME-L (production), Lin-CAE (linear-consistency baseline), and DAC (44 kHz codec).

All operations are performed in latent space only and then decoded: reconstruction D(E(x)), interpolation D(w1(α)·z_A + w2(α)·z_B), and algebraic separation D(z_mix − z_other). The reference in every experiment is the corresponding audio-domain operation. Sections 1–3 are presented without scores, so you can judge by ear; in Section 4 the number under each player is the SI-SDR of exactly the audio you hear (scale-invariant, dB). Only one player plays at a time — starting one stops the previous.

4.Algebraic source separation (in-distribution synthetic mixtures)

Mixtures are built artificially from the autoencoders’ own training corpus (VCTK voice + FSD50K/FMA interference, natural raw sums, no renormalisation): mix = voice + other. The voice is then extracted without any separator network by pure latent algebra, D(z_mix − z_other) (oracle access to the interfering stem’s code, as in the paper’s in-distribution protocol). Under each model’s separation we also provide its own reconstruction ceiling — D(E(voice)) — which shows that the baselines’ failure is algebraic, not a codec-quality artifact: their ceilings are fine, their subtractions are not.

Protocol & selection. All models run on identical clips; every latent operation is followed by that model’s own decoder. Displayed cases were selected from the paper's full quantitative evaluations. All files are peak-normalised to 0.95 when converted to 16-bit PCM. The only flagged case is an actual output-level failure: “⚠ volume reduced” marks a model whose separation output was dangerously loud and was attenuated for safe listening — the level failure itself is part of the result. Lin-CAE decodes stochastically; a fixed decode seed is used for reproducibility.
